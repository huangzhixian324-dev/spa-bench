## SUMMARY
Transcriptomic predictors of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) response are benchmarked on small, incompatible cohorts with inconsistent endpoints, obscuring whether conclusions reflect algorithms or design choices. SPATBench isolates three such design choices across six methods and five immunotherapy-treated cohorts (196 patients). Switching from a molecular surrogate (cytolytic activity) to a clinical endpoint (RECIST v1.1) in the same patients collapses every method whose features overlap the endpoint-defining genes (ElasticNet-Var 0.985 to 0.465; GEP 0.968 to 0.503; IMPRES nearly unchanged), 3.5× the largest within-cohort method gap, while two clinical response definitions on identical patients move predictions by at most 0.084. MI- and variance-based feature selection yield 27%-overlapping gene sets at indistinguishable performance. The Circularity Detection Score (CDS) flags the circular endpoint far above its cohort-matched null, yet its categorical labels prove non-specific. No trainable method survives FDR correction on clinical endpoints at n ≤ 43, whereas at n = 73 (Gide) ElasticNet-MI is significant within its cohort family (p = 0.0232). Both tools are open-source and Docker-reproducible.

## INTRODUCTION

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting PD-1, PD-L1, and CTLA-4 have transformed oncology, achieving durable responses in melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and other malignancies [1,2]. Yet only 20–30% of unselected patients achieve objective responses, motivating intensive efforts to develop transcriptomic biomarkers for stratification [3–6]. 
Multiple gene-expression-based prediction methods have been proposed: the T-effector gene expression profile (GEP) [3], TIDE [4], IMPRES [5], and a growing number of machine learning (ML) models [6–8]. Each was evaluated on different cohorts, preprocessing, response definitions and protocols — a fragmentation that produced a literature in which nearly every method appears superior, but only within its own developmental context [9,10]. 
Three structural features of the field make method rankings unreliable: (i) public immunotherapy cohorts with pre-treatment RNA-seq are small (typically n < 50), limiting power [11–13]; (ii) response can be defined via clinical endpoints (RECIST v1.1), molecular surrogates (cytolytic activity [14]), or progression-based endpoints (PFS), each capturing different biology; and (iii) methodological choices such as feature selection are rarely treated as experimental variables. 
Several recent efforts have benchmarked immunotherapy prediction methods across multiple cohorts [15–18]: Liang et al. [15] found limited cross-cohort generalizability for nine predictors, COMPASS [16] cross-cancer improvements on 1,133 patients, the ICLR 2026 benchmark [17] domain-specific models outperforming general-purpose ones across 35 tasks, and EXPRESSO [18] 20 signatures across 3,729 patients; a re-analysis of 17 tumour-microenvironment scoring methods found none reliable across cancer types [19] (Supplementary Table S2 compares their published AUROCs with ours). All hold methodology fixed and compare methods; our approach is orthogonal — we hold methods fixed and systematically vary methodological choices, asking which design decisions, rather than which algorithms, determine benchmark outcomes. 
Here we present SPATBench, a computational framework and benchmark resource asking: *given that rankings are unreliable at available sample sizes, which methodological choices contribute most to that unreliability, and by how much?* Our contributions are:

1. An open-source benchmark framework enforcing patient-stratified cross-validation and standardized preprocessing across six prediction methods and five cohorts.
2. Empirical isolation and quantification of three design choices — response definition, feature selection, and endpoint circularity — with effect-size comparisons against method-level variation.
3. A Circularity Detection Score (CDS) for pre-benchmarking endpoint assessment — a null-referenced continuous diagnostic whose categorical labels are advisory only; the validated deliverable is a reporting convention whose binding rule is the declaration of endpoint-defining genes, with CDS as its quantitative companion rather than an independent gate — evaluated on 5 real endpoints, 5 synthetic scenarios, 60 pseudo-endpoints, one retrospective external consistency check (Auslander et al. 2018), and published benchmark studies.
4. A complete permutation test matrix with high-resolution validation for borderline cases, showing that no trainable method is distinguishable from label shuffling on any clinical endpoint at n ≤ 43 after FDR correction.
5. A power-analysis-derived checklist of minimum benchmarking standards. All evidence is retrospective on public cohorts; the response-definition result is demonstrated in melanoma and bladder carcinoma, with NSCLC replication an open gap.

## RESULTS

### Design Choice 1: Response Definition Exerts a Larger Effect Than Method Choice

The Riaz 2017 cohort provides two response definitions on the same patients and expression matrix.

**Table 1. Riaz 2017: RECIST (n = 42) versus cytolytic (n = 43) response definitions (v33, HGNC-mapped).**

| Method | RECIST AUROC (n=42) | Cytolytic AUROC (n=43) | ΔAUROC | Type |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|--------|------|
| ElasticNet-MI | 0.569 [0.370–0.755] | 0.968 [0.909–1.000] | −0.399 | ML |
| ElasticNet-Var | 0.465 [0.208–0.704] | 0.985 [0.954–1.000] | **−0.520** | ML |
| TIDE | 0.466 [0.222–0.723] | 0.470 [0.282–0.653] | −0.004 | Signature |
| IMPRES | 0.613 [0.381–0.835] | 0.660 [0.493–0.811] | −0.047 | Signature |
| GEP | 0.503 [0.305–0.710] | 0.968 [0.915–1.000] | −0.465 | Signature |
| PD-L1 (CD274) | 0.545 [0.330–0.760] | 0.868 [0.750–0.960] | −0.323 | Biomarker |

Switching response definitions transforms all ML methods from apparently state-of-the-art (Var 0.985 on cytolytic) to near-chance on RECIST (0.465; Figure 1); GEP collapses with them (0.968 → 0.503) because the cytolytic endpoint is defined by GZMA/PRF1, both members of the GEP signature. IMPRES — whose fifteen checkpoint-gene relations share no genes with the endpoint definition — is nearly insensitive (0.660 → 0.613, Δ = −0.047; permutation p = 0.032 on cytolytic, 0.154 on RECIST, each against its own null). Within the Riaz RECIST endpoint the largest between-method ΔAUROC is 0.147 (IMPRES vs TIDE); the largest between-endpoint effect (0.520) exceeds it 3.5× (Supplementary Table S14). The 0.520 contrast uses the benchmark (seed-42) split; across 10 stratified seeds the k = 2 RECIST mean is 0.389 ± 0.105 (Supplementary Table S16) — the collapse carries ≈0.1 seed SD while its direction and scale are stable. 
**k-fold robustness:** The Riaz RECIST endpoint uses only 2-fold CV (9 responders). Re-running ElasticNet-Var with k = 3 and k = 5 (10 stratified seeds per k) keeps all RECIST AUROC below 0.60 (k = 3: 0.397 ± 0.064; k = 5: 0.398 ± 0.088; Supplementary Table S16; results/benchmark/v33/riaz_recist_kfold_v33.json) — not an artifact of the k = 2 constraint.

**External replication (pre-declared):** The effect replicates on an independent cohort — Liu 2019 [20] (n = 122 annotated samples; DFCI anti-PD-1 melanoma; independent of all five benchmark cohorts; iAtlas harmonization; the pre-specified substitution for GSE274975). On the same patients and expression matrix, the cytolytic-surrogate endpoint (GZMA/PRF1 median split — the Riaz construction) yields GEP 0.959 and ElasticNet-MI 0.956, RECIST 0.535 and 0.538 (Δ = +0.425 and +0.418); IMPRES, whose features exclude the endpoint genes, retains the most RECIST signal (0.601) and is the only nominally significant fixed scorer (p = 0.027; BH q = 0.108), mirroring the Hugo headline result; no trainable CI excludes 0.5 on RECIST. CDS flags the surrogate HIGH (0.949 vs null mean 0.566) and places the clinical endpoint above its null mean (0.721 vs 0.591) — the Gide genuine-biology signature (Table S4b). Full results in Supplementary Table S19; pipeline in scripts/e1_gse274975_replication.py.

**Cross-cancer replication (pre-declared):** The effect replicates in a non-melanoma tumour type — IMvigor210 [21] (metastatic urothelial carcinoma, n = 297 response-annotated samples; anti-PD-L1; iAtlas harmonization). On identical patients, the cytolytic-surrogate endpoint yields GEP 0.967 and ElasticNet-MI 0.951, RECIST 0.613 and 0.657 (Δ = +0.354 and +0.294); PD-L1 shows the same pattern (0.863 → 0.566, Δ = +0.297). On the clinical endpoint, GEP is the only fixed scorer surviving FDR (p = 0.0025; BH q = 0.010) — a weak effect (AUROC 0.613) detectable only at this sample size (Power Analysis); IMvigor210 is an application cohort of the GEP [3], so this significance reflects cross-cohort signal, not cohort of origin. CDS cleanly separates the two endpoints: the surrogate is HIGH (0.953 vs null mean 0.514) while RECIST falls below its null mean (0.434 vs 0.492) — the cleanest separation to date, confirming the Gide prognostication conflation is cohort-specific. Full results in Supplementary Table S20.

**Clinical–clinical contrast (non-circular):** One public cohort carries two clinical response definitions on the same patients: Liu 2019, whose iAtlas harmonization annotates both RECIST and PFS. We defined durable clinical benefit (DCB) as progression-free at ≥6 months and excluded samples censored earlier. The two definitions agree substantially (κ = 0.669; 48 RECIST responders, 58 DCB). Across all six methods, switching between them moves predictions by at most 0.084 (ElasticNet-Var), roughly sixfold below the circular collapse (0.520 on Riaz) and below the largest within-cohort method gap on Liu RECIST (0.216, IMPRES vs TIDE). This is consistent with the circularity account, which predicts that neither clinical definition injects gene–label dependency into the feature space; as a single melanoma cohort with a complete-case construction, E5 is supportive rather than dispositive. CDS places both endpoints in the genuine-biology band (0.721 and 0.726 against null means of 0.622 and 0.549). IMPRES is the only method permutation-significant under both definitions (p = 0.016 and 0.005), consistent with checkpoint-pair biology rather than a definition artifact. Full results in Supplementary Table S26.

**Mechanism:** The cytolytic score is operationally defined by GZMA and PRF1 expression [14]. Learning methods predict it from co-expressed immune-infiltration genes, and GEP does so best (0.968) because GZMA/PRF1 are GEP members. RECIST — not gene-defined — removes that leverage (0.503, 0.465), while IMPRES, whose checkpoint-gene relations do not overlap the endpoint genes, stays near chance on both definitions. CDS flagged the cytolytic endpoint HIGH (0.954) because endpoint-defining genes sit inside the feature space.

On the HGNC-mapped Riaz matrix, TIDE is near chance for both endpoints (RECIST 0.466, cytolytic 0.470), consistent with the tidepy implementation checked at source (classification = correlation['TIDE'] < vthres, default vthres = 0).

### Design Choice 2: Feature Selection Determines Biological Interpretation

Within identical ElasticNet models on Hugo 2016 (n = 28, RECIST) we compared variance- and MI-based feature selection.

**Table 2. ElasticNet on Hugo 2016 (n = 28): MI versus variance feature selection.**

| Metric | MI-based | Variance-based | Interpretation |
|--------|----------|----------------|----------------|
| AUROC (5-fold nested CV) | 0.631 | 0.528 | Not distinguishable (cross-seed Δ −0.008 ± 0.050, Table S12) |
| Gene overlap (top-500) | — | 27.0% (135/500) prescreen convention; 2.4% (12/500) full-data MI | Overlap is convention-dependent (v33; two-stage prescreen is the pipeline's actual selection path — MI is computed within the top-2000 variance prescreen; the full-data MI variant is the alternative convention) |
| Spearman ρ (OOF scores) | — | 0.75 (p < 0.001) | Highly correlated predictions |
| Observed ΔAUROC (MI − Var) | +0.103 (benchmark split) | −0.008 ± 0.050 (10 seeds, Table S12) | Includes zero |
| Permutation p (n=60 full pipeline) | 0.131 | 0.344 | Neither distinguishable from chance |

Under the corrected, leakage-free implementation (v33) the two strategies achieve indistinguishable performance and highly correlated out-of-fold predictions (Spearman ρ = 0.75; Supplementary Figure S2); their top-500 gene sets overlap by 27% under the pipeline's two-stage prescreen convention (2.4% under the full-data MI convention — the overlap's dependence on the convention is itself a design-choice effect; Supplementary Figure S3). Neither variant is distinguishable from chance by permutation (p = 0.13 and 0.34 at n = 28; Table 2). Feature selection at n = 28 therefore changes the selected gene sets — and the biological narrative — without measurably changing predictive performance (cross-seed stability: Supplementary Figure S7; Table S12).
Learning curve analysis (Supplementary Figure S6; Table S13) shows the expected small-sample behavior: point estimates remain near chance at every subsample size while variance inflates steeply as n decreases (across-seed SD 0.211 at n = 8 vs. 0.038 at n = 28); the n = 8 point (~6 training samples per fold at k = 5) characterizes estimator variance rather than performance. 
### Design Choice 3: Endpoint Circularity Can Be Detected Before Benchmarking

**Relation to Design Choice 1.** The response-definition effect is mechanistically an instance of endpoint circularity: the cytolytic surrogate is operationally defined by genes inside the feature space. Design Choice 1 measured its *magnitude* within patients; Design Choice 3 asks whether circularity can be recognised *before* any benchmark is run, and develops the diagnostic. The corresponding non-circular contrast is measured directly: between the two clinical definitions on Liu 2019 (RECIST versus PFS-derived DCB), endpoint-switch deltas reach only 0.084 across six methods — the collapse is specific to gene-defined surrogates, not to clinical-definition disagreement (Results; Table S26).

'ML AUROC Range' = trainable methods only (ElasticNet MI/Var), v33 values. Riaz RECIST is reported on the HGNC matrix (0.409); the archived 0.685 was computed on the pre-v33 Entrez matrix.**

CDS places the only circular endpoint far above its random-label null (Riaz cytolytic 0.954 vs null mean 0.566, max 0.842; Figure 2) — exactly where a GZMA/PRF1-defined endpoint must sit — with ML methods reaching 0.968–0.985. The clinical endpoints sit at or inside their nulls (Hugo 0.449 vs 0.586; Riaz RECIST 0.409 vs 0.560; Lauss 0.781 vs 0.578, max 0.866). Gide 2019 (Table S4b) returns 0.852, above its null maximum (0.800): the declared genes genuinely track response there (GEP 0.830, q < 0.001) — genuine biology that CDS v1.x cannot separate from circularity, so the categorical thresholds do not carry the validated claim. The validated claims are the null-referenced ordering above and the procedural rule that any gene-defined surrogate, of any program type (Supplementary Table S18), is circular by construction. 
**Within-study consistency check:** CDS scores for Hugo, Riaz and Lauss preceded the corresponding ML benchmarks; all sat at or within their nulls (Table 3), and no trainable AUROC exceeded 0.70 on them. The benchmarks therefore constitute a subsequent verification of the CDS readings — a within-study check, not prospective validation. External prospective validation remains open (Limitations).

**External consistency check:** The Auslander et al. (2018) study provides an independent validation scenario. IMPRES was trained on Auslander's data and validated externally on Hugo 2016 (RECIST), achieving AUROC 0.83 in the original paper (0.795 in our benchmark). Had CDS existed then, it would have returned 0.449 for Hugo RECIST (below its cohort-matched null mean of 0.586) — correctly indicating a non-circular endpoint and consistent with IMPRES reflecting checkpoint-pair biology rather than endpoint-gene dependency.

### Integrated Multi-Cohort Results

Across the four clinical-endpoint cohorts (Hugo, Lauss, Gide, Riaz RECIST; full AUROC/CI table in Supplementary Table S22, plotted in Supplementary Figure S8), the methods swap ranks: IMPRES is best on Hugo (0.795) and Riaz RECIST (0.613), GEP on Gide (0.830), and PD-L1 on Lauss (0.780), while neither ElasticNet variant leads any column. The between-method spread reaches 0.508 (Jung 2019: GEP 0.786 vs TIDE 0.278), so cross-cohort rankings conflate cohort effects with method effects — a spread that partly reflects deployment choices, since the Jung TIDE cell is computed under the tidepy default threshold convention (Methods) and sits at the two-sided edge of its permutation envelope (two-sided p ≈ 0.08); either reading illustrates that cross-cohort rankings mix design, deployment and method effects. All trainable-method bootstrap CIs on clinical endpoints at n ≤ 43 overlap 0.5, and no method ranking is claimed.
On the fifth cohort-endpoint, Jung 2019 DCB (n = 27, NSCLC; corrected labels 1 = durable clinical benefit, 6/27; Supplementary Table S23), TIDE scores far below chance (0.278); with only 6 DCB responders this deviation sits inside the permutation null envelope (p = 0.952), and the value is reported as computed under the tidepy default threshold convention rather than re-tuned to the cohort.
### Survival Analysis: AUROC Alone Does Not Capture Clinical Utility

On Hugo 2016, RECIST response strongly stratifies overall survival (OS; log-rank p = 0.0003 with true events — 26 patients / 12 deaths, with the local-versus-iAtlas metadata validation reported in Supplementary Table S24; Supplementary Figure S4), whereas IMPRES — the best AUROC method on this cohort — does not (KM p = 0.554). Cox regression with z-standardized signatures (HR per 1 SD) confirms the dissociation (IMPRES HR = 0.631, 95% CI [0.325–1.227], p = 0.175; GEP HR = 1.03, 95% CI [0.562–1.887], p = 0.923): AUROC for binary response prediction does not guarantee clinically meaningful survival stratification.
Clinical-utility metrics for ElasticNet-Var on Hugo (v33 recomputed; full values in Supplementary Table S9) point the same way: decision-curve net benefit is positive but consistently below treat-all (0.345 vs 0.405 at p_t = 0.10; 0.259 vs 0.330 at 0.20; 0 at 0.50), and reclassification gains are absent (NRI = −0.113, p = 0.658; IDI = −0.029). The argument is not an artifact of analyzing a weak method. IMPRES, the only FDR-significant method on this cohort (AUROC 0.795), fails the same way as a decision tool: at the 0.5 threshold it labels 92% of patients responders (sensitivity 0.923, specificity 0.267), its net benefit coincides with treat-all at the 10% and 20% thresholds, and its reclassification metrics do not exceed the random baseline (NRI = −0.20, IDI = −0.073; Supplementary Table S9). Its score is an uncalibrated fraction, so these metrics reflect threshold behavior; either way, the ranking advantage of the cohort's only surviving method does not extend to decision utility.
The local Lauss matrix contains no OS metadata, so the archived Lauss survival row could not be regenerated under the v33 protocol and is excluded (archived values retained in Supplementary Table S9 for continuity).
**Uncertainty on the effect-size comparison.** The 3.5× is a descriptive ratio of benchmark point estimates (Δ = 0.520 against a same-endpoint method gap of 0.147), not a bootstrap quantity. The inferential anchor is the Δ interval, which excludes zero under both hyperparameter constructions on the full 42-patient RECIST-evaluable set (frozen: Δ = 0.343, bootstrap 95% CI 0.097–0.584; tuning: Δ = 0.386, CI 0.163–0.598; 1,000 patient resamples, seed 42). The ratio itself is unstable because its denominator is small and volatile at this n (frozen-track ratio 11.98, CI 0.46–14.32; tuning-track constructs give ratios centred near 2.6, CI 0.52–3.18), so the 3.5× is reported as descriptive only and the existence claim rests on the Δ intervals. ### Power Analysis: Minimum Sample Size Requirements

**One-sample detectability.** Table S25 quantifies two-method comparisons; one-sample statements (AUROC exceeding 0.5) are calibrated with the Hanley-McNeil single-sample variance (normal approximation, one-sided α = 0.05; `results/benchmark/v33/power_onesample_v33.json`). At n ≤ 43, 80% power requires a true AUROC of 0.74–0.79 (Riaz RECIST 0.75; Hugo 0.74; Lauss 0.76; Jung 0.79), whereas at n = 73 (Gide) it falls to 0.66: effects genuine at n = 73 in the 0.60–0.70 range sit below the detection floor at n ≤ 43 — which is what the permutation matrix shows.

At n = 25–43 the minimum detectable ΔAUROC is 0.21–0.23 (Supplementary Table S25; curves in Supplementary Figure S9). Pairwise comparison at ΔAUROC = 0.10 (80% power) requires ≈130 patients under the most favorable correlation assumption (paired, r = 0.75) and ≈260–510 under weaker or no correlation (power_table_v33.json); n ≥ 50 is therefore a minimum practical floor, not a sufficient condition (Table 9); the floor is anchored to detectability — a true AUROC of 0.68 first becomes detectable at 80% power at n ≈ 55 (40–55% responder fractions), and the smallest effect observed to survive FDR (ElasticNet-MI, 0.662 on Gide) requires n ≈ 71–79, a range that brackets the Gide cohort size (73). 
**Table 9. Minimum requirements for reliable immunotherapy prediction benchmarking.**

| Requirement | Current Practice | Recommendation |
|-------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Patients per cohort | n = 25–43 | n ≥ 50 minimum floor; ≈130 for pairwise ΔAUROC = 0.10 at 80% power (paired, r = 0.75; ≈260–510 under weaker correlation) |
| Independent cohorts | 1–2 | ≥3 with consistent endpoint definitions |
| Response definitions | Mixed (RECIST, PFS, surrogate) | ≥1 clinical endpoint; surrogates labeled with defining genes |
| Feature selection | Often unreported | ≥2 strategies pre-specified; report overlap and cross-seed stability |
| Circularity check | Not performed | Compute CDS; report continuous value; calibrate thresholds on synthetic data |
| Non-learning reference baselines | Often omitted | Include non-learning baselines as negative controls (IMPRES, which does not overlap endpoint genes; GEP's own collapse instead demonstrates the mechanism, since its genes contain GZMA/PRF1) |
| Permutation test | Rarely performed | Full permutation matrix; FDR correction; ≥5000 permutations for borderline cases† |
| Statistical reporting | AUROC only; p < 0.05 | Exact p-values; 95% CIs; effect sizes; cross-fold SD at n < 50 |
| Reproducibility | Variable | Docker container + Zenodo archived data |

†Trainable-method cells use 2,000–5,000 full-pipeline shuffles (each shuffle re-fits the pipeline, ≈60–90 s; all eight clinical trainable cells extended in 2026-09-18, four cells to 5,000); ≥50,000 applies to fixed scorers, and the resolution-hardening protocol is described in Methods. 
**Priority guidance:** The three largest-variance levers are (1) at least one clinical endpoint alongside any molecular surrogate, (2) pre-specified multi-strategy feature selection with overlap reporting, and (3) non-learning reference baselines, with IMPRES as the negative control and GEP's own collapse as the mechanism demonstration; the remaining recommendations add rigor but address smaller-magnitude variation.

## DISCUSSION

### A predictive account of when gene-defined surrogates mislead

The three modules compose into one statement: a gene-defined surrogate misleads in proportion to (i) the immune-infiltration-driven co-expression supply linking endpoint genes to the feature space (high in melanoma, low in COAD-like contexts — the TCGA gradient below), (ii) the overlap between declared endpoint genes and the feature space (complete for the cytolytic surrogate, absent for IMPRES), and (iii) sample size, which sets the detectability floor (nothing below n ≈ 43) and the power to separate genuine signal from construction (single methods from n ≈ 73, two-method comparisons from n ≈ 130; Table S25). These anchors form a testable claim — in a low-infiltration tumour type the endpoint-switch collapse should shrink toward the method gap even at adequate n — and the 36-cell matrix and CDS nulls make it checkable. The TCGA anchor is prognostic and its SKCM–COAD contrast partially conflates cancer type with metastatic-versus-primary composition (the SKCM signal is metastatic-carried; Table S15), so it conditions rather than demonstrates the account. The account also predicts that two clinical definitions of the same construct should barely move predictions; the Liu 2019 RECIST-versus-DCB contrast provides direct support for this (largest endpoint-switch Δ = 0.084 across six methods, below the largest within-cohort method gap; Table S26), while remaining a single-cohort, complete-case comparison. 
SPATBench asks a different question from existing benchmarks [15–18], which hold methodology fixed: which design choices cause that unreliability at available sample sizes, and does the answer convert into design rules? The answer: response definition exerts a larger effect (largest within-cohort Δ = 0.520, ElasticNet-Var) than any between-method difference on the same RECIST endpoint (max Δ = 0.147); across cohorts the between-method spread reaches 0.508 (Jung), which is why the endpoint effect must be demonstrated within-cohort — at n = 43, *how you define response matters more than which algorithm you choose*. The mechanism ports across two further cohorts with full endpoint-switch replication (Liu 2019, melanoma; IMvigor210, bladder carcinoma — the latter a cytolytic-to-RECIST switch on 297 patients with a clinical RECIST endpoint, largest Δ = 0.354 against a same-cohort method gap of 0.180, CDS 0.953 versus 0.434), plus the Gide surrogate-construction control (CDS 0.955); all use the same author-constructed cytolytic surrogate, so these establish cross-cancer portability of the circular construction on clinical endpoints, not that clinical RECIST rankings differ across every tumour type (no suitable NSCLC RECIST cohort exists; Limitations); the published-study audit (Tables S17, S20) establishes cross-study consistency of the reading on the computable cohorts. Of the Table 9 rules, the genuinely new ones from this study are requiring a clinical endpoint alongside any gene-defined surrogate, declaring endpoint-defining genes, and computing CDS; the remaining rows reinforce standard reproducibility hygiene. Beyond immunotherapy, any transcriptomic prediction task using a gene-defined surrogate carries the same circularity risk, and CDS generalizes to any expression matrix paired with a binary label. 
We use "design choice" rather than "confounder" throughout: the three variables are under experimental control — *variance-dominating factors in small-sample benchmarks* — and should therefore be controlled by experimental design, not merely adjusted for statistically. 
### The Response Definition Problem

The cytolytic → RECIST collapse (largest Δ = 0.520, ElasticNet-Var) generalizes: any molecular surrogate operationally defined by genes in the feature space creates a circular prediction task. The corresponding rules are distilled in Table 9 (clinical endpoint required; surrogates labeled with their defining genes; CDS computed; non-learning baselines included); one rule is not captured there: for endpoints with CDS > 0.80 (recalibrated HIGH threshold), assume ML methods are learning the endpoint-defining genes until independent clinical-endpoint validation proves otherwise. 
### Feature Selection as a Narrative Design Choice

The substantial overlap of MI- and variance-selected gene sets (27% of the top-500) with highly correlated out-of-fold predictions (ρ = 0.75) shows that feature selection determines the biological story even when AUROC is statistically indistinguishable at n = 28. The reporting rules are in Table 9 (≥2 strategies pre-specified; overlap reported; cross-seed stability at n < 50); the one addition: accompany biological interpretation with sensitivity analysis — overlap reporting is the single most impactful check, directly revealing whether conclusions are strategy-dependent. 
### On Method Context and Generalizability

Our data are consistent with method performance being inherently context-dependent [15,17]: IMPRES's AUROC ranges from 0.795 (Hugo) to 0.583 (Jung DCB), and GEP's from 0.446 (Hugo) to 0.830 (Gide). IMPRES's context-sensitivity is consistent with Carter et al. (2019), who showed IMPRES failing in an independent melanoma cohort (AUROC 0.52–0.55) [22] ; the inherited feature-selection-bias caveat is in Limitations. Benchmark studies should therefore report each method's known developmental limitations alongside performance. 
### Independent Prognostic Evidence: TCGA Cancer-Type Gradient

The TCGA analysis is **prognostic, not predictive** — univariate Cox models of overall survival without clinical covariates (stage, age) — and cannot validate treatment-response prediction; it provides large-scale independent evidence that immune-signature behavior reflects method–context interaction.
**v33 correction (data-provenance audit).** Re-running the pipeline against the repository's current data reproduced three of the four archived cohorts and revealed two provenance defects, disclosed here rather than silently re-used. First, SKCM sample-type policy: the archived analysis used all tumour samples (n = 426 of 444; 367 metastatic, 76 primary); the restored all-tumour policy yields n = 441 patients (212 deaths), with **11/12 immune signatures are significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction** (HR 0.795–0.928; BH p ≤ 2.7e-3) — the *stromal* signature is the only non-significant one and the only one with HR > 1 (1.086, BH p = 0.090), an internal negative control consistent with immune-specific rather than generic prognostic biology; the signal is also not a stage- or age-proxy — in multivariate Cox models adjusted for age and AJCC stage, 8/12 signatures remain BH-significant with stronger protective HRs (0.569–0.704; Supplementary Table S15). Second, the local COAD expression file was not TCGA-formatted and could not support the archived COAD result (n = 588, 119 deaths), so v33 withdrew it and re-acquired the cohort from the GDC current index (STAR counts, GENCODE v36, primary tumours; scripts/build_coad_gdc.py), yielding n = 458 patients, 102 deaths, with **0/12 signatures significant (all HR ≈ 1)** — independently reproducing the archived finding (the GDC STAR source differs from the other three cancers' cBioPortal RSEM — disclosed; it does not affect within-cancer statistics). The original report used nominal p < 0.05 only; BH-adjusted values within each cancer type strengthen the SKCM conclusion by surviving FDR correction at 48 tests. The TCGA validation comprises 2,491 patients (SKCM 441 + BRCA 1,082 + LUAD 510 + COAD 458; 12 signatures × 4 cancer types; data/tcga/tcga_cox_all.json).
A pronounced cancer-type gradient emerges (Supplementary Figure S11; Supplementary Table S15; BH within each cancer type): 11/12 signatures significant in SKCM, 3/12 in BRCA (CD8 T cells, B cells, TLS), 1/12 in LUAD (B cells) and 0/12 in COAD — an ordered decrease (intermediate steps rest on one to three signatures each; descriptive, not a calibrated dose–response). The B-cell signature is significant in SKCM, BRCA and LUAD but not COAD (HR 0.904/0.885/0.873 vs 1.153); CD8 T-cell and TLS are significant in SKCM and BRCA only; IFNG-response only in SKCM. Immune signatures point protectively in SKCM/BRCA/LUAD while COAD HRs hover at or slightly above 1 (e.g. CD8 T cells 1.177) — COAD reproduces the archived finding that immune infiltration is not prognostic there. No time-dependent AUC is reported under v33 (archived 0.33–0.60 not regenerated). This pattern matches Usset et al. (2024), who showed that thousands of published biomarkers collapse into five latent factors [23]. Full methods in Supplementary Note S4.  
### CDS: Current Status and Validation Roadmap

CDS is itself subject to the same validation standards we recommend for benchmarking tools — an epistemological tension we acknowledge. Its current status:

- **What CDS can do:** Quantify endpoint–gene association on a null-referenced scale: the circular Riaz cytolytic endpoint sits far above its null (0.954 vs null mean 0.566, max 0.842) while the clinical endpoints sit at or within their nulls (Hugo RECIST 0.449, Riaz RECIST 0.409; Table 3). Provide a lightweight (<10 s) diagnostic requiring only an expression matrix, binary labels and declared endpoint genes (pip installable; NumPy/SciPy/scikit-learn only). Support the procedural circularity rule — any endpoint defined by genes in the feature space is circular by construction, whatever its score — shown program-agnostic by the negative-control stress test (Table S18). Retrospective application to the Auslander et al. (2018) external validation of IMPRES on Hugo is consistent with genuine signal rather than circular inflation (Results) [5,30]. The same reading extends to the published benchmark studies whose cohorts are computable — Auslander et al. on Hugo, and Jiang/Mariathasan via IMvigor210 (Table S17a–b): in every computable cohort the cytolytic positive controls sit far above their nulls while the clinical endpoints sit at or within theirs (an independent 10-replicate null run preserves the ordering — Tables S17/S19/S20); the Chowell and Liang multi-cohort designs remain unverified, and the Van Allen CTLA-4 cohort [24] remains expression-inaccessible. (The Gide 2019 value in Table S4b is a v33 recomputation of a score never stored at analysis time.)

- **Practical pre-benchmarking workflow:** (1) Declare which genes, if any, operationally define the endpoint; a gene-defined surrogate is circular by construction — switch to a clinical endpoint or accept that ML AUROC will be inflated by endpoint–gene co-expression. (2) Compute CDS on the declared genes before training any model. (3) Report the continuous CDS value against a 200-replicate random-label null on the same cohort and feature space; a value far above the null maximum flags strong gene–label association — circular construction or genuine biology, distinguishable only by provenance. (4) Treat the shipped categorical labels (HIGH > 0.70, MODERATE 0.30–0.70, LOW ≤ 0.30) as advisory: Table S18 shows they carry no specificity among structured endpoints. No ML training is required. 
- **What CDS cannot yet do:** Prospectively validate on independent multi-endpoint cohorts (none is publicly available with the required dual-endpoint design). Provide well-calibrated categorical thresholds (provisional; recalibrated from synthetic data with n = 30 null replicates). Distinguish among MODERATE-risk endpoints (C3 saturation limits discrimination in the 0.50–0.80 range). Be computed on cohorts where the raw expression data is not publicly accessible. 
**CDS: Known Failure Modes.** (1) **Undeclared endpoint genes (false negatives):** undisclosed molecular surrogates evade detection; if CDS is LOW but ML AUROC > 0.80, suspect undeclared endpoint-gene dependency. CDS v2.0 will add a hypothesis-free mode that permutation-tests the full feature-label MI distribution. (2) **Label noise (false negatives):** label misclassification attenuates gene-label correlations and may return LOW risk; compare CDS between the suspect labels and a gold-standard endpoint on the same cohort — a large discrepancy suggests label-quality issues. (3) **C3 saturation in immune-dominant contexts (loss of discrimination):** in highly immune-infiltrated cancers (melanoma, NSCLC), C3 saturates at 1.000 on every real cohort and all 60 pseudo-endpoints (Table S18), leaving C1 and C2 as the sole discrimination sources; CDS v2.0 will use a permutation-based C3 to estimate the null distribution of feature-space enrichment.

- **Validation roadmap:** (1) Re-calibrate C3 using pathway-stratified feature selection (CDS v2.0). (2) Validate on external multi-endpoint cohorts as they become available (the pre-declared candidate GSE274975 was investigated and disqualified). (3) Third-party replication on independent benchmark endpoints before full evaluations. (4) Pre-registration of CDS scores, enabling prospective validation against subsequently observed AUROC values. 
### Limitations

1. **Sample size:** All 95% CIs for the trainable methods on clinical endpoints at n ≤ 43 overlap 0.5 (the sole fixed-scorer exception is IMPRES on Hugo — the headline positive result, which does not bear on method ranking among trained models; Lauss PD-L1 is nominal-only, q = 0.058). IMPRES's CI width (0.350 on Hugo) quantifies the genuine uncertainty at n = 28. No ML method is statistically superior for n ≤ 43. 
2. **Response-definition evidence base:** The cytolytic→RECIST collapse was demonstrated on Riaz 2017, replicated on two independent cohorts — Liu 2019 (melanoma, n = 122; Table S19) and IMvigor210 (bladder carcinoma, n = 297; Table S20) — using the same author-constructed surrogate, and is k-fold robust (Supplementary Table S16). The collapse magnitude (largest Δ = 0.520) may be cohort-specific: where immune infiltration is weaker (in TCGA, 0–1 of 12 signatures reach FDR-adjusted significance in LUAD and none in COAD), the effect should attenuate because less immune co-expression is available to leverage. The clinical–clinical contrast bounds the non-circular component: RECIST versus PFS-derived DCB on Liu 2019 moves no method by more than 0.084 (Table S26), so the demonstrated collapse is attributable to the circularity channel rather than to clinical-definition disagreement. NSCLC replication remains an open gap: the pre-declared candidate (GSE274975) lacks clinical endpoints, and a documented screen of the ten iAtlas harmonizations (no NSCLC study exists there) and of GEO found no suitable NSCLC ICI cohort with dual endpoints and public expression data. 
3. **CDS calibration, program-agnosticity and C3 formulation:** The thresholds are provisional, and the negative-control stress test (Supplementary Table S18) shows the labels have no specificity among structured endpoints: all 60 pseudo-endpoints score HIGH (0.79–0.95), and 20–36% of 200 random-label replicates exceed the shipped 0.70 boundary on every real cohort (`cds_nulls_200_v33.json`) versus ≈3% on the synthetic calibration null — the shipped labels overstate specificity on real data by an order of magnitude. CDS's validated content is the continuous value against a cohort-matched random-label null; circularity protection is procedural (declare the endpoint-defining genes). The Gide 2019 RECIST value (0.852 vs null max 0.800; GEP 0.830 marks the genuine biology) quantifies the core limitation: CDS v1.x cannot separate genuine biology from circularity. C3 saturates at 1.000 on all real cohorts because highly variable genes in bulk tumour RNA-seq are predominantly immune-related; the composite is bounded to [0.40, 1.00] there, making the shipped LOW tier (≤ 0.30) mathematically unreachable. With C3 saturated, CDS on real bulk data reduces to an affine transform of C1 and C2 (0.4 + 0.3·(C1+C2)); the constant offset shifts the real-cohort distribution upward relative to the synthetic calibration — which is why the categorical tiers do not transfer to real cohorts (20–36% of real random-label replicates exceed 0.70 versus ≈3% on the synthetic null) and why the cohort-matched null, which absorbs the offset, is the validated reading. 
4. **Data access and cross-cohort validation:** Earlier versions could not evaluate IMPRES, GEP, or TIDE on Jung 2019 (Ensembl IDs without HGNC mapping); as of v33 the HGNC-mapped matrix is used for all methods, and the Jung label orientation was corrected (the release codes 1 = no durable benefit — the inverse of every other cohort; all analyses use 1 = DCB). Leave-one-cohort-out validation remains precluded by gene-identifier heterogeneity across cohorts — a barrier that is itself a finding; CDS's data-access requirement (public expression matrix + labels) serves as a de facto transparency enforcement mechanism. 
5. **Clinical biomarker proxies:** TMB and PD-L1 IHC results use gene expression proxies (constant baseline and CD274 expression, respectively).

6. **Cohort composition:** The immunotherapy cohorts are predominantly melanoma (169/196 patients), and one of the five is adoptive T-cell therapy rather than ICI (Methods). The TCGA analysis provides cross-cancer validation (2,491 patients, 4 cancer types), but CDS calibration and the response-definition collapse are demonstrated in melanoma only. Gide 2019 includes both monotherapy and combination-therapy arms, introducing treatment heterogeneity. 
7. **Learning curve caveat:** The n = 8 subsample analysis (k = 5, ~6 training samples per fold) is diagnostic rather than a precise estimate.

8. **IMPRES interpretative caveat:** IMPRES's Hugo performance (0.795, q = 0.017) may partially reflect inherited feature-selection bias from the original training protocol (34% non-random repeats) [22]; the balance between genuine checkpoint biology and inherited bias is unresolved.

### Methods Evaluated

**Gene signatures (no training required):** GEP (the 8-gene Ayers IFN-γ/T-effector signature: CD8A, GZMA, GZMB, IFNG, CXCL9, CXCL10, PRF1, TBX21 [3]; the circularity channel analyzed in this study is specific to gene sets containing GZMA/PRF1 — the 18-gene expanded GEP does not contain them and would not be expected to show the same collapse), TIDE (tidepy v1.3.9; the implementation was checked at source — classification = correlation['TIDE'] < vthres, default vthres = 0 — consistent with its near-chance scores on both Riaz endpoints, Table 1), IMPRES (the 15 canonical pairwise checkpoint-gene relations of Auslander et al. [5]; the relations were decoded from the authors' published feature set (github.com/noamaus/IMPRES-codes, FEATS.mat) and cross-checked against the 9 pairs named in the paper text (9/9 match); the remaining 6 relations are not independently verified beyond the authors' released file. For a sample, the IMPRES score is the fraction of the 15 logical relations (e.g., CD40 > PD-1) whose expression ordering holds, linearly rescaled when features are missing, exactly as described in the original Methods. No retraining is performed, mirroring the original design in which features were selected once on neuroblastoma spontaneous-regression data.)

**Gene expression biomarker:** PD-L1 (CD274 expression; proxy for IHC — real IHC scores unavailable in public metadata).

**Machine learning (training required):** ElasticNet logistic regression (sklearn saga solver, penalty = 'elasticnet') with variance-based feature selection (top-500 genes by expression variance in the training fold), ElasticNet with mutual information-based feature selection (top-500 genes by MI with the response label in the training fold), and XGBoost gradient boosting. ElasticNet hyperparameters (C, l1_ratio) were tuned via 3-fold inner cross-validation within each training fold; the selected hyperparameters were frozen during permutation testing, as is standard.

**Supplementary baselines:** TMB (constant 0.5; real mutation data unavailable) [28]. Immune deconvolution signatures (14 cell types, 43 unique genes; Supplementary Table S10) [29].

The six benchmarked prediction methods span four categories. All feature selection used only training-fold data.

### Evaluation Protocol

Patient-stratified k-fold cross-validation with identical splits across all methods within each cohort. Ten metrics were computed. Here we report AUROC as the primary metric with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (1,000 iterations) [30]. Expression-scale, preprocessing and cross-validation-strategy sensitivity analyses on Hugo 2016 (ElasticNet-MI) are provided in Supplementary Figure S5 and Tables S7 and S8. AUROC, AUPRC and bootstrap CIs for every method–cohort cell are provided in Supplementary Table S9, with clinical-utility metrics (Brier score, DCA, NRI, IDI) for a representative trainable cell; per-fold values are fully determined by the archived split files (Supplementary Table S1). For cohorts with n ≤ 28, we additionally report cross-fold standard deviation (SD) as a stability metric. 
### Permutation Testing

Full permutation tests were performed for all trainable method–cohort pairs. Exact p-values = (number of permuted AUROCs ≥ observed + 1) / (n_permutations + 1). Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction was applied across all tests within each cohort [31].

**Inclusion logic:** Two protocols are used, matched to each method class. (a) Fixed scorers — IMPRES, GEP, TIDE, and PD-L1 (CD274 expression) — produce scores that are independent of the labels, so the full-pipeline label-shuffle null is mathematically identical to shuffling labels against the fixed out-of-fold scores; each cell therefore uses 50,000 prediction shuffles (seconds of compute, exact to 1/50,001). This equivalence was verified by running both protocols on Hugo IMPRES. (b) Trainable methods — ElasticNet-MI and ElasticNet-Var — are re-fit end-to-end under every label shuffle (per-fold feature selection with frozen hyperparameters), with a per-cell number of shuffles recorded in Supplementary Table S11. XGBoost is excluded from benchmark reporting (degenerate on all clinical endpoints; its only non-degenerate AUROC sits on the circular cytolytic endpoint — development-phase evaluation only). Re-examination (v33 protocol): under the current protocol XGBoost is not degenerate — re-runs produce non-degenerate predictions (Hugo 0.554, Lauss 0.493, Jung 0.611, Riaz-RECIST 0.434, Gide 0.718, and 0.991 on the circular cytolytic endpoint; all six cohorts re-run; `results/benchmark/v33/xgb_exclusion_evidence_v33.json`); it shows no signal on any clinical endpoint at n ≤ 43, so the negative claim is unaffected. A 1,000-shuffle full-pipeline permutation test of the Gide re-run (frozen hyperparameters, the Table S11 protocol) gives p = 0.004 and q = 0.007 in the within-cohort family recomputed over seven cells, with the six original Gide q-values unchanged or more conservative: XGBoost is the third detectable trainable signature at n = 73, and the detectability-onset reading now covers all three trainable methods tested. XGBoost remains outside the reported 36-cell matrix because the development-phase runs were not retained under the current protocol; the re-run and permutation evidence files are retained for audit (permutation record: `results/benchmark/v33/nc_cells/Gide_2019_cBio__XGBoost.json`; xgboost 3.4.1) even though the prediction tables are not.

**Permutation resolution:** p-values are exact given the recorded per-cell shuffle count: 50,000 for fixed scorers (minimum detectable p = 2 × 10⁻⁵) and 2,000–5,000 full-pipeline shuffles for the trainable methods, where the compute cost of re-fitting under every shuffle (≈60–90 s per shuffle) is the binding constraint. The initial budget ranged from 60 to 1,000 shuffles, under which the smallest achievable exact p at 60 shuffles (1/61 ≈ 0.016) would have imposed a resolution ceiling inside a six-test cohort family. All eight clinical trainable cells were therefore extended to a uniform minimum of 2,000 shuffles (Hugo and Lauss MI/Var to 5,000; Jung and Riaz-RECIST MI/Var to 2,000) in a pre-registered resolution-hardening run (`scripts/extend_nc_cells_5000.py`, merged by `scripts/merge_nc_cells_ext.py`); no cell changed its significance verdict. The one borderline trainable cell, ElasticNet-MI on Gide 2019 (n = 73), had earlier been extended to 5,000 full-pipeline shuffles (checkpointed resumable protocol, frozen hyperparameters): the observed AUROC under this protocol is 0.662 (0.033 above the benchmark 0.629 — frozen-versus-tuned hyperparameters, the same deviation class disclosed in the Supplementary Table S11 footnote), giving p = 0.0232 and within-cohort BH q = 0.028 — **FDR-significant**. ElasticNet-MI therefore joins ElasticNet-Var as a detectable trainable signature on Gide (n = 73), strengthening the reading that Gide is the smallest sample size at which trainable transcriptomic signatures become detectable on a clinical endpoint; our headline claim is unchanged, since it is scoped to n ≤ 43, where — at the extended resolution — no trainable method survives (the smallest within-cohort trainable q at n ≤ 43 is 0.0716, Lauss ElasticNet-MI/Var; the smallest q on any cell is 0.058, Lauss PD-L1), independently corroborated by bootstrap 95% CIs that all overlap 0.5 (Supplementary Table S9).

### Multiple Comparison Framework

Three levels of inference: (i) Exploratory: AUROC point estimates with 95% bootstrap CIs reported without multiplicity correction (descriptive quantities). (ii) Permutation testing: all evaluated method–cohort pairs with within-cohort FDR correction. (iii) Pairwise comparisons of AUROCs via paired bootstrap tests of ΔAUROC (bootstrap implementation. The analytic DeLong covariance method [32] was considered but the bootstrap version was used throughout — we refer to these as paired bootstrap tests, not DeLong tests). The Bonferroni threshold (p < 0.05/36 ≈ 0.0014) is unattainable at current sample sizes — we treat this inaccessibility as a finding. Because family definition is an analysis degree-of-freedom, Supplementary Table S21 reports the full 36-cell matrix under three family definitions (within-cohort 6×6; global 36; method-class-stratified 24+12): the primary conclusions are family-robust, and the four family-dependent cells are disclosed there. 
### Effect-Size Comparison Framework

To compare the relative influence of methodological choices against method choice, we computed the range of AUROC values attributable to each source on the Riaz 2017 cohort: (a) between-endpoint ΔAUROC for each method; (b) between-method ΔAUROC within each endpoint; (c) between-feature-selection ΔAUROC within ElasticNet. We report these as effect-size comparisons rather than variance-component percentages, which are unreliable with limited independent data points. 
Between-method spreads differ across cohorts, so the endpoint effect must be compared with method effects at the same level of control. Within the same patients and the same RECIST endpoint (Riaz), the largest between-method ΔAUROC is 0.147 (IMPRES 0.613 vs TIDE 0.466), so the largest between-endpoint effect (Var: 0.985 → 0.465, Δ = 0.520) exceeds it by 3.5×; the same comparison yields ≈2.2× on Liu 2019 (0.425/0.195) and ≈2.0× on IMvigor210 (0.354/0.180) — the direction replicates, with a smaller ratio. The effect is further supported by non-overlapping bootstrap confidence intervals. The cytolytic-endpoint AUROC CIs for ElasticNet-Var ([0.954, 1.000]) and ElasticNet-MI ([0.909, 1.000]) do not overlap the RECIST-endpoint CIs ([0.208, 0.704] and [0.370, 0.755] respectively), and the same non-overlap pattern holds for the external replications (Liu 2019: cytolytic GEP [0.928, 0.986] vs RECIST [0.427, 0.642]. IMvigor210: cytolytic [0.948, 0.981] vs RECIST [0.536, 0.686]). Across different cohorts the between-method spread can be as large as 0.508 (Jung: GEP 0.786 vs TIDE 0.278), so a cross-cohort comparison cannot support the claim; the controlled within-cohort comparison on Riaz is the appropriate design, because it fixes patients, treatment, and expression data and varies only the endpoint definition. 
### Power Analysis

Minimum detectable ΔAUROC at 80% power (α = 0.05, two-sided) as a function of sample size and responder proportion, using the Hanley–McNeil asymptotic variance of the AUC (Mann–Whitney U) for a paired two-method comparison on the same patients (baseline AUC 0.5) with AUC correlation r = 0.75 — the upper end of the correlation observed between method pairs on out-of-fold scores (Supplementary Table S12). The computation is archived as `scripts/power_table_v33.py` with a machine-readable sensitivity grid over r ∈ {0, 0.5, 0.75} (`power_table_v33.json`); Supplementary Table S25 reports the r = 0.75 (most favorable) column, rounded to 0.01, and regenerates exactly from the archived script. Weaker correlation assumptions inflate the required n roughly two- to four-fold. Full power curves in Supplementary Figure S9. 
### Circularity Detection Score (CDS)

CDS quantifies endpoint-feature dependency as a weighted composite: **C1 (30%):** mean MI between endpoint-defining genes and the response label, normalized as min(C1_MI / 0.05, 1). **C2 (30%):** mean |Spearman ρ| between endpoint-defining genes and the response **C3 (40%):** min(1, R/10), where R is the ratio of the 90th-percentile MI of the top-500-variance features to the median MI of 500 random features. The composite is CDS = 0.3·C1n + 0.3·C2 + 0.4·C3. These weights and normalization constants are those of the released tool (v1.2.1; source and packaging at https://github.com/huangzhixian324-dev/cds and in the Zenodo archive); every CDS value in this manuscript is exactly reproducible from them. One calibration caveat applies wherever a categorical label is quoted: the HIGH boundary (0.70) was calibrated on synthetic nulls, where 3% of replicates exceed it, whereas 20-36% of real-cohort random-label replicates do (Limitations); the validated reading is the continuous value against its cohort-matched null (CDS scores are identical between v1.1.0 and v1.2.1 — v1.2.1 changes only the output semantics: it adds a null-referenced reading mode, a caveats block shipping the calibration limits below, and condition-specific guidance). The CDS concept builds on earlier work by Zhao et al. (2011, BMC Genomics), who introduced a "consistency degree" metric quantifying correlation between disease endpoints and gene expression profiles [33]. We note two properties of this formulation: (a) C3 saturates at 1.0 for all evaluated endpoints and random-label replicates — highly variable genes in bulk tumor RNA-seq are predominantly immune-related, so the top-to-random MI ratio consistently hits the clipping ceiling regardless of endpoint circularity, and (b) we evaluated a re-weighting (C1 40% / C2 40% / C3 20%) as a candidate refinement, but the released tool and all reported values use the 30/30/40 formulation Permuting C3's role (v2.0) is planned to improve MODERATE-range discrimination. 
**Synthetic data calibration:** To validate CDS independently of our three real endpoints, we generated synthetic expression data (n = 200, 5,000 genes) with controlled circularity levels (0–100% endpoint-gene signal, with 50 independent biological-signal genes as non-circular signal). CDS ranks the five scenarios monotonically (CDS 0.697 < 0.820 < 0.830 < 0.832 < 0.845; Supplementary Table S4d). Scenarios with CDS ≥ 0.80 show inflated AUROC (0.691–0.845), while the pure-random scenario scores 0.697 — above the level of an unstructured label, reflecting C3 saturation. 

**Retrospective external consistency check:** The Auslander et al. (2018) study provides an independent validation context for CDS. In that study, IMPRES was trained on Auslander's own data and validated externally on Hugo 2016 (RECIST endpoint), achieving AUROC = 0.83 in the original paper (0.795 in our benchmark). If CDS had been computed before that validation was performed, it would have returned CDS = 0.449 (MODERATE under the shipped thresholds, and below its cohort-matched random-label null mean of 0.586), indicating that the Hugo RECIST endpoint is not circular — any observed AUROC reflects genuine biological signal in the IMPRES gene pairs, not endpoint-gene dependency. This counterfactual application of CDS to a published external validation scenario constitutes a retrospective consistency check: CDS's reading (non-circular endpoint → AUROC not inflated by endpoint-gene dependency) is consistent with the observed outcome (IMPRES achieved significant AUROC = 0.795 on Hugo RECIST, permutation p = 0.0029). The same logic applies to Lauss 2017 (ACT) (CDS = 0.781, MODERATE under the recalibrated boundary; no trainable AUROC exceeds 0.70, no inflation). For Gide 2019 no CDS value was stored at analysis time; the v33 recomputation (Supplementary Table S4b) returns CDS = 0.852 — the C1/C2 components read the strong association between the declared genes (GZMA/PRF1) and the clinical label in this large melanoma cohort as endpoint-gene dependency, i.e. the known inability of CDS v1.x to separate genuine immune-biology signal (GEP AUROC = 0.830, q < 0.001) from circularity. CDS correctness claims are therefore restricted to the endpoints with stored scores.

**Null distribution, stress test, and reporting convention:** We generated 30 independent replicates of random binary labels to estimate the CDS null distribution. Mean null CDS = 0.50 (SD = 0.09), 95th percentile = 0.65, 99th percentile = 0.71. Approximately 3% of random endpoints were flagged as HIGH under the shipped threshold (CDS > 0.70) due to C3 saturation at 1.0 — a known limitation of the current C3 formulation; no random endpoint achieved CDS < 0.30. We disclose that these 30 null replicates were generated **after** the four real benchmark endpoints had been inspected; threshold calibration is therefore retrospective, not prospective, and categorical risk labels must be treated as advisory. We deliberately ship a single threshold set (HIGH > 0.70, MODERATE 0.30–0.70, LOW ≤ 0.30) — identical in the manuscript, in the released pip package, and in all archived result files — so that users replicating our analysis obtain exactly our labels. A more conservative HIGH threshold of 0.80 (false-positive rate 0/30 on the synthetic null) is reported for transparency and will be adopted in CDS v2.0 together with a permutation-based C3. We recommend reporting the continuous CDS value; categorical risk assignments are advisory.

 To characterise what CDS measures, we generated 30 pseudo-endpoints per cohort from real gene programs [34] — ECM/stromal (collagens, fibroblast, endothelial, pericyte and basement-membrane markers), tumour-purity/proliferation (cell cycle, E2F/MYC targets, glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation) and lineage/signalling programs (melanocytic differentiation, keratinocyte basal, hypoxia, interferon, antigen presentation) — each dichotomised at the median of the program-mean expression, the same construction as the cytolytic surrogate, with the program's own genes declared as endpoint genes. All 60 pseudo-endpoints (Gide 2019, n = 73; Hugo 2016, n = 28) scored above the HIGH boundary (CDS 0.792–0.946): CDS flags any operationally gene-defined surrogate regardless of program identity. Random-label nulls computed per endpoint on the real feature spaces (10 replicates each; Table 3) show that the shipped 0.70 threshold also captures 3/10 null replicates at n = 28. CDS is therefore validated as a continuous, null-referenced measure of endpoint–gene association; the categorical HIGH label carries no specificity among structured endpoints, and the protection it enables is procedural — declaring the endpoint-defining genes identifies the circular construction itself. Full design and per-endpoint results in Supplementary Table S18 and Note S6. 
 CDS values are reported alongside the mean, maximum and 95th percentile of a 200-replicate random-label null computed on the same cohort and feature space (Table 3); the archived 10-replicate nulls give the same readings for every endpoint. A CDS far above the null maximum indicates that the declared genes track the label beyond chance — expected for operationally gene-defined endpoints (circularity), but also possible for genuine biology when the declared genes are true correlates of response (the Gide 2019 case, Table S4b). The two are distinguishable only by endpoint provenance, which is why the declaration of endpoint-defining genes is the primary control. 
**Epistemological note:** We recognize the inherent tension: CDS is itself subject to the same validation standards we recommend for benchmarking tools — prospective evaluation on independent multi-endpoint cohorts, transparent reporting of limitations, and third-party replication. Endpoint–feature overlap of the kind CDS detects is one of the leakage classes formalized in the machine-learning reproducibility literature [35]. To facilitate this, CDS is released as an open-source, independently installable package with a documented API and bundled synthetic calibration data. Its current status is experimental, and its provisional thresholds should be refined as more multi-endpoint cohorts become available.

### IMPRES Implementation

IMPRES uses the canonical implementation decoded from the original authors' released feature set: 15 fixed logical relations (list in Supplementary Note S5), score = fraction fulfilled, no retraining. This matters because IMPRES on Hugo 2016 is the headline positive result of this study — it is the only FDR-significant non-circular-endpoint result at n ≤ 43 (Supplementary Table S11); that result must rest on the published algorithm, not on an ad hoc variant. On the canonical implementation, IMPRES on Hugo 2016 yields AUROC = 0.795 (permutation p = 0.0029 at 50,000 prediction shuffles under the fixed-scorer protocol; q = 0.017 after BH). We note that Carter et al. (2019) identified potential training-set bias in the original IMPRES feature selection (34% non-random repeats) [22]. The extent to which IMPRES's Hugo performance reflects genuine biology versus inherited feature selection bias is unresolved; we report the AUROC as observed and flag this interpretative caveat.

### QUANTIFICATION AND STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Permutation testing uses full-pipeline label shuffles with frozen hyperparameters; exact p-values are (ge + 1)/(n + 1). Within-cohort multiple testing uses Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q < 0.05). AUROC 95% CIs use the paired bootstrap (1,000 iterations, the convention used throughout and in Table S9); correlated-AUROC comparisons likewise use the paired bootstrap rather than DeLong.
